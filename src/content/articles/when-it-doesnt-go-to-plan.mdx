<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Missed one run.</strong> Let it go. Don't make it up, don't add it to another day.</li>
<li><strong>Missed most of a week.</strong> Repeat that week. Don't jump ahead to stay on schedule.</li>
<li><strong>Missed two weeks or more.</strong> Drop back to the start of the current block and rebuild.</li>
<li><strong>A week feels genuinely too hard</strong> — not unpleasant, but leaving you flat for days. Repeat it at 80% of the listed distances before moving on.</li>
<li><strong>Forced to choose which run to protect:</strong> long run first, medium-long second, quality last. Volume is what makes the marathon; the Tuesday session is what makes it fast, and fast is the thing you can afford to lose.</li>
</ol>

<Note variant="stop" label="Stop the session">
<p>Sharp or pinching pain anywhere. Pain that grows as you run. Anything that changes how you move. Chest pain or dizziness. Pain above roughly 3 out of 10. Anything still present 48 hours later means you go back a week — not forward with a better attitude.</p>
<p>At 66 km a week the tissues that fail first are the calf, the Achilles, the shin and the plantar fascia, roughly in that order. Bone stress injuries announce themselves as a small point of pain that gets worse during a run rather than warming up. That one is not negotiable — stop and get it imaged.</p>
</Note>
<Note variant="go" label="Carry on">
<p>A dull ache that warms up and clears within ten minutes and is gone the next morning. Heavy legs on a Sunday after a Saturday long run. General fatigue in weeks 11 and 13. Being bored on easy runs — that means you're doing them right.</p>
</Note>
